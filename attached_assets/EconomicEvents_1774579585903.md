Timeline of Major Economic Shocks (2015–2025)
Brexit Referendum (2016): The UK's vote to leave the EU defied market expectations, causing the British pound to plunge to its lowest level against the dollar in 30 years.
US-China Trade War (2018–2019): A series of tit-for-tat tariffs between the world's two largest economies created massive policy uncertainty. The S&P 500 declined 6% in 2018—a rare drop without a recession—as investors feared global supply chain disruptions.
COVID-19 Pandemic (2020–2022):
The Crash (March 2020): The S&P 500 plunged 34% in just weeks, and the Dow Jones experienced its worst losses since 1987.
The Recovery & Boom: Unprecedented fiscal stimulus (like the $2T CARES Act) and near-zero interest rates fueled a massive rally in technology and "stay-at-home" stocks like Zoom and Tesla.
Russia-Ukraine Conflict & Energy Crisis (2022): The invasion reshaped global trade for oil and gas, sending energy prices to record highs and triggering a global inflationary shock.
The Great Rate Hike Cycle (2022–2024): To combat 40-year high inflation, central banks (led by the US Fed) raised interest rates at the fastest pace in decades, moving the federal funds rate from nearly 0% to over 5%.
Banking Crisis of 2023: The collapse of Silicon Valley Bank (SVB), Signature Bank, and First Republic marked the largest bank failures since 2008, causing a brief but intense liquidity panic.
Artificial Intelligence Boom (2023–2025): The launch of ChatGPT ignited a massive "AI-driven capex cycle," driving the "Magnificent Seven" tech stocks to all-time highs and accounting for a significant portion of S&P 500 gains.
